## Methodological change: cycle first, drum set second

For traditions whose rhythmic identity does not originate in a Western kick/snare/hi-hat backbeat, the canonical score now stores the source rhythmic logic in metadata before it is orchestrated on the nine-track drum-set UI.

Examples:

- Arabic iqa‘at: `dum` = low/resonant function, `tak` = high/dry function. The drum-set score maps these functions to kick and cross-stick/snare, but the cycle remains the canonical identity.
- Balkan aksak: ordered short/long units (2 and 3 eighth-note units) are stored as `metricGrouping`; the odd meter alone is not considered sufficient identity.
- Hindustani taal adaptations: cycle/vibhag groupings and low/high tabla functions are retained; the drum-set realization is explicitly labelled an adaptation.

## Africa

CAN-173…188 were rebuilt with distinct drum-set structures. Afrobeat, Highlife, Kpanlogo, Ewe/Agbekor 12/8, Fanga, Mbalax, Jùjú, Soukous, Makossa, Bikutsi, Mangambe, Mbaqanga, South African House, Amapiano, Sega and Maloya no longer fall through a shared generic Latin/House template.

Berklee's *African Rhythms for Drum Set* is retained as the main pedagogical reference because it explicitly studies traditional African rhythms, direct drum-set adaptation and contemporary Afro-pop application:
https://college.berklee.edu/courses/ilpd-355

Amapiano is deliberately represented as an adaptation with a broken house kick and a low-tom proxy for log-drum rhythmic function. The metadata does not claim that a tom is a literal log drum.

## Arabic rhythmic cycles

CAN-192…198 are rebuilt around cycle-first low/high stroke functions rather than a generic backbeat. Maqsum, Sa‘idi, Malfuf, Ayyub, Baladi/Masmudi Saghir, Masmudi Kabir and Ciftetelli now have separate drum-set skeletons and explicit `sourcePercussionRoles` metadata.

MaqamWorld remains the primary taxonomy reference:
https://www.maqamworld.com/en/iqaa.php

Long cycles such as Masmudi Kabir and Ciftetelli are represented across the application's two-bar 4/4 phrase model rather than being flattened into a one-bar pop beat.

## Balkans / Eastern Europe

A major bug was corrected: **Balkan 7/8 previously fell through to a 9/8 generator**. CAN-199…202 now encode their signatures explicitly and store asymmetric grouping order:

- Balkan 7/8: **2+2+3**
- Balkan 9/8: **2+2+3+2**
- Balkan 11/8: **2+2+3+2+2**
- Čoček / 9/8 Dance: **2+2+2+3**

This follows the aksak principle of unequal short/long units rather than treating all odd meters as interchangeable.

References:
- https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/yearbook-of-the-international-folk-music-council/article/abs/on-rhythm-in-rumanian-folk-dance/303B8DC39543C9789026B7186337972D
- https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/DF1897CA3A024BFF3AB73392CD28822E/9780521553490AR.pdf/The_Balkan_Languages.pdf?event-type=FTLA

## South Asia

CAN-203…206 are no longer generated by one generic kick/snare template.

- Bhangra / Dhol Drum-Set: low/high dhol-derived dialogue remains explicitly an adaptation.
- Teentaal: the **16-beat, 4+4+4+4** cycle is represented over the two-bar application phrase.
- Keherwa/Kaharwa: **8 beats, 4+4**, with sam/khali structure documented.
- Dadra: **6 beats, 3+3**, with sam/khali contrast documented.

References:
- https://www.raganet.com/Issues/8/tabla8.html
- https://digitabla.com/reference/tals-and-thekas/kaharawa-tal/
- https://digitabla.com/reference/tals-and-thekas/dadra-tal/

These are pedagogical drum-set projections. They do not claim to replace tabla/dhol stroke technique, phrasing, timbre or improvisational grammar.
